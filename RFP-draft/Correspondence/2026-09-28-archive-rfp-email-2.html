Subject: RFP: Archival assessment and master plan | San Francisco | 200 linear feet + personal objects

Hello West_Arch colleagues,

I'm Theo Armour, an architect and software developer in San Francisco. I seek an experienced archivist for a paid collection assessment and archival master plan covering roughly 200 linear feet of family and personal papers, along with artworks, objects, and legacy digital media.

This may be my final design project. My long-term aim is a well-described digital archive, publicly accessible where appropriate.

This initial engagement covers assessment and planning. Please also describe how you would lead subsequent triage, digitization, and transfer to archival repositories, coordinating specialists from your own network. I want to be an excellent client, not the project manager.

The collection runs from the 1700s to today.

One thread is family history, with close relatives including ambassador Norman Armour, George Armour, President of the Chicago Board of Trade and Founder of the Art Institute of Chicago, Albert Burrage, founder of Chile’s Chuquicamata copper mine, Walter Lowrie, first translator of Søren Kierkegaard's work into English and Russian Prince Serge Koudacheff, whose Dutch Masters collection is now being traced by a researcher.

The other thread is my own working life, spanning architecture, software, and cultural institutions:

  • Renovating old buildings with a tenant/worker cooperative in South London and swimming the English Channel in the 1970s, then designing 12 Hong Kong subway stations in the 1980s.
  • Selling and demonstrating computer-aided design software to highly receptive audiences in China, Taiwan, and Korea, then designing three AutoCAD releases for Autodesk in the 1990s that were prime movers in the change from drafting on paper to designing on computer screens.
  • Working with web and open-source communities, Three.js and web-based 3D graphics, and now exploring AI applications in architecture and digital archiving.
  • Serving on the boards of the Peggy Guggenheim Collection in Venice and the San Francisco Ladies Protection and Relief Society.

There are letters, photos, slides, negatives, large-format drawings, business records, and a good deal of legacy digital media as well as numerous objects and museum-quality works of art.

The attached RFP has the details. Proposals are due November 30, 2026. I'm glad to talk before you submit.

With thanks,
