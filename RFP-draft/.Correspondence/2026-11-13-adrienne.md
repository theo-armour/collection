# 2026-11-13 Adrienne

Hello Theo,
Thank you for your note. I would still advertise the opportunity, but I would narrow the RFP to hiring an archives consultant to assess the collections and help you approach potential repositories.

I would ask for two deliverables.

An archival appraisal and collection assessment that describes the materials, evaluates their research value and condition, and recommends repositories to approach. For each institution, the consultant should explain why the materials might fit its collecting scope or complement holdings it already has.

A concise proposal you can share with prospective repositories. It should give them enough information to assess their interest, including the proposed terms of transfer, your goals for public access, and the funding and time you are willing to contribute.
The proposal could explain that you are willing to fund digitization and participate in the project. Depending on the repository’s capacity and priorities, the work could be handled by existing staff or by a dedicated project archivist funded by you and hired and supervised by the institution. The archivist could work directly with you, with clear roles and responsibilities. For example, you could contribute contextual information, help draft the finding aid, and enrich the metadata for digitized materials. The repository would give final approval and ensure that the description follows DACS and other applicable standards.

Once a repository expresses interest, you can work out the specifics together. The repository will be responsible for stewarding the collection, so it needs to guide decisions about staffing, costs, timing, vendor selection, technical specifications, and what portion of the collection it can make publicly available through its digital asset management system. I would keep the RFP focused on the assessment and proposal so the receiving institution can help shape the work from the outset.
