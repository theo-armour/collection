# Adrienne Correspondence


Hello Theo,
Thank you for sharing the revised RFP. Your goals are clearer, but my main recommendation remains: the initial engagement should help you define a feasible project and identify potential institutional partners. The RFP still prescribes too much of the work that should follow that assessment.

I would hire an archivist to assess the collections’ research value, scope, condition, organization, and preservation needs according to archival best practices; research institutions with related holdings or collecting interests; and prepare the information those institutions would need to evaluate an acquisition. This would also mean clarifying the offer: donation or sale, conditions of transfer, your priorities for access, and the funding you could contribute.

I would remove the detailed requirements for processing levels, metadata, digitization specifications, scanning workflows, and platforms at this stage. Those are professional recommendations that should emerge from the assessment and conversations with potential partners. Some of the prescribed steps do not align well with archival theory and practice.

The proposed separation of physical placement and digital access also needs reconsideration. A receiving institution may want to manage the originals and their digital representations together, using its own description, systems, and access policies. Deciding those arrangements in advance could limit your placement options. Broad digital access can be a central goal you bring to those conversations, along with an offer to fund processing, description, and digitization. The scope and timing would need to be worked out with each institution, taking its capacity, priorities, and backlog into account.

There is a relevant precedent from my own experience. In 2016, I worked at the Computer History Museum on a one-year processing contract funded by the donor of five software-related collections. We also digitized substantial material using the museum’s digitization lab. The museum could be worth exploring for your technology history papers. The family papers would require separate placement research.

I would not begin with the assumption that nobody wants the materials. Assessment and outreach should test that first. If suitable repositories cannot be found, the consultant could develop an alternative plan, including investigating whether Internet Archive could support the digitization and access you envision. That would still require a plan for archival description, potentially a finding aid, and the disposition of the originals.
My concern with the proposed initial engagement is therefore its deliverables, rather than primarily its price. I would also shorten the cover email to focus on hiring someone to assess the collections, investigate partnerships, and help determine how your goals can realistically be achieved.

Finally, I cannot undertake paid consulting work on this project because it would present a conflict of interest. While I am happy to continue to consult on this project, please do not name me or SFPL as a formal collaborator on this project - providing archival recommendations is part of my job and is available to everyone.

Thank you for your engagement!
Best,
Adrienne
