## The one big change

Release 0.1 never states the long-term outcome. Your 2026-10-22 email to Adrienne states it perfectly in four lines:

> Placement, with one condition: an openly available digital copy. The end state: I hold no physical objects, only the digital collection.

**RFP 0.2 should open with this.** Put a short "Outcome" section right after the Overview, built from the 10-22 email:

* Digital images of everything worth keeping, open license (CC BY 4.0 / MIT), hosted on free long-lived platforms (Internet Archive, Permanent.org, Wikimedia Commons, GitHub).
* Originals placed source by source: existing collections first (Princeton for Norman Armour and Walter Lowrie), museums by donation with qualified appraisal, collectibles sold, the rest given away or recycled after imaging.
* Working assumption: nobody wants anything, so everything gets imaged regardless.
* End state: no physical objects, only the digital collection.

This single section answers Adrienne's main objection and lets every respondent scope and price accurately. It also reassures individual consultants that you are *not* asking them to build and run an independent archive.

## Notes on the five questions for Adrienne

Heads-up first: **questions 3 and 4 (numbers, credit/compensation) were already asked in the 10-22 email**, along with the consulting question. If she hasn't replied, fine to fold them into the new email — but acknowledge the repeat ("in case my last email was buried...") rather than asking cold.

1. **Other archival groups.** Beyond the Society of California Archivists list: the SAA (Society of American Archivists) Independent Archivists Section and SAA Connect open forums; Archives Gig (archivesgig.com — widely read for paid project work); Northwest Archivists; the Academy of Certified Archivists directory for direct outreach. West_Arch is already covered by the 0.1 email. Adrienne will know which are worth the trouble.

2. **Timing.** Sending early November with interest by end of November is reasonable. The risk is the holidays: ask for *interest* by Nov 30 but set the *proposal* deadline in mid-January, or you'll get rushed proposals or none.

3. **Numbers.** Walkthrough at up to $250 for ~2 hours is fine and the gesture of paying it is a strong signal. Phase 1 at $2,000–$5,000 looks low: at typical consulting-archivist rates ($85–150/hr), that's roughly 15–50 hours, and a survey of two storage units (~200 linear feet) plus a written master plan with digitization specs, costs, and schedule is plausibly 40–80 hours. Consider $5,000–$10,000, or keep hours×rate and present $2k–5k as a floor, not a ceiling. Adrienne's answer to this is the one to trust.

4. **Credit / compensation.** Already asked 10-22; repeat gently as above.

5. **Unknown unknowns — candidates to ask her about.**
   * **Digital estate:** who controls the GitHub/Internet Archive/Permanent.org accounts after you? The "I hold only the digital collection" end state needs a named successor or the archive dies with the logins.
   * **Copyright of third-party material:** letters *received* are copyrighted by their writers; photos by unknown photographers. You can't CC-BY what you don't own — the plan needs a rights-triage step.
   * **Tax mechanics of donations:** IRS qualified appraisal + Form 8283 for gifts over $5,000, paid by you, arranged before transfer.
   * **Deeds of gift** and institutional acquisition timelines — Princeton et al. can take a year+ to say yes.
   * **Privacy of living people** in recent papers (already touched in Roles, worth flagging to her).
   * **Insurance/liability** while an archivist handles museum-quality items in storage units.
