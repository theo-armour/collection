# Archiving Terminology

## Archival bond

>The archival bond refers to the inherent relationship between records within a collection that arises from their creation, accumulation, and use by the originator. This bond helps maintain the context and provenance of the records, ensuring that the relationships between documents are preserved and understood.

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archival_bond

## Collection surveying

>The first step in archival processing is to survey the collection. The goal of a survey is to gain an understanding of the originator, determine the context of the creation of the collection, to observe the material's overall size and scope, to ascertain if the collection has access limitations, to locate any existing finding aids submitted with the collection, and to discover any underlying organizational scheme in the collection or record group

## Metadata

>Metadata (or metainformation) is data (or information) that defines and describes the characteristics of other data.[1] It often helps to describe, explain, locate, or otherwise make data easier to retrieve, use, or manage. For example, the title, author, and publication date of a book are metadata about the book. But, while a data asset is finite, its metadata is infinite.

* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metadata

## _Respect des fonds_

>_Respect des fonds_ is an archival principle that records of a single originator should be kept together and not mixed with records from other originators. This principle helps maintain the context and provenance of the records, ensuring their authenticity and reliability.

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respect_des_fonds
