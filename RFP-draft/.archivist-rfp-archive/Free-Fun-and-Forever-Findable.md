## Personal Interest

The other half of the stuff in the boxes tends to relate to my own life. And I call this "Three Ways Theo Helped Change the World." In the 80s, I taught China how to build factories and universities using computers. In the 90s, the software I designed pushed engineers and architects from drawing on drawing boards to designing on computer screens. In the 2010s, the software I promoted elevated the web browser from delivering static 2D content into supporting interactive 3D apps.

The archiving effort would be to add veracity and background to these conjectures. In many ways, whether my conjectures are true or not makes almost no difference. Sometimes I think of these conjectures as a deep truth, and other times I think of them as a tongue-in-cheek wordplay.

What is more interesting, maybe more important ultimately, is that recording the normal efforts of normal people doing normal things is as important as recording the acts of people who excel at defeating others in war or whatever.

## Deliverables

Will it be a:

> Comprehensive digitization or item-level description of the collection?

The initial effort is a comprehensive digitization of whatever can be digitized, by one means or another.

During the process of digitization, simple cataloging and descriptions can be ascribed where readily doable.

As and when this initial project is well underway or completing on its own, then the process of describing the contents may begin. And that would be done by me, by AI, with third-party help and by the historians of the future.

## Collection Creation

* Triage and sorting of originals will require talented archivists
* Initial triage requires one or more reasonably experienced archivists who could open up a box of "treasures", carry out the triage and decide where and how the treasure should be processed.

The boxes contain scans, photos, slides and negatives, letters and cards, postcards, medical records, ID documents, taxes, insurance documents, business documents, faxes, instructions, software, manuals, data, papers, articles, clippings, publications, notebooks, business cards, data repositories online and offline, websites, emails, social media, audio recordings.

The archivist should be able to perhaps identify the source, the age, whether it belongs in a known collection or not.

## Collection Digitization

* Multiple digitizing specialists will be required.

The result will be collections of items to be sent to particular scanning specialists. Flat paper documents that can be put through a sheet feeder, large format architectural drawings that need to be photographed, slides and negatives that need to be scanned with a film scanner, objects that need to be photographed or 3D scanned, and so on.

## Collection Management

* Identifying a landing zone for digitized files (currently mostly DropBox, Google Drive and GitHub)
* Processes for feeding these to ultimate destinations such as Archive.org and Permanent.org.
* Identifying, cataloging, tagging, summarizing, and more will require both human and AI input
* Text, images, timelines, genealogies, and various media will be dealt with
* Once photographed or digitized in some way, there are numerous items of furniture and personal objects that will need new homes.
* Where suitable, I hope to be able to donate these to organizations that would appreciate them.
* Other objects can be sold on eBay or wherever and be made available to the collectors of the future.

## Future Goals

In a world where every tweet, every Instagram story, email message, restaurant visit, term paper, 1040 and more is recorded and stored forever somewhere, the meaning of "archive" is open to something we cannot really even imagine today.

A goal here is to take all that we've learned in preserving physical and paper archives and upgrade these skills with all the newly available capabilities.

If nothing else, a simple first goal is to help inform people about organizations like the Internet Archive, Permanent.org, and the Society of American Archivists.

A third aspect: I live at the epicenter of where all newfangled technologies are in the process of changing the world. We are reimagining the world as we speak. And so, why not reimagine the ways in which we treat the past at the same time?
