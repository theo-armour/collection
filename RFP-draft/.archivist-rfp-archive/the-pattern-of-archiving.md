## Pattern: Use the force: Call upon the experts

## The Problem: complexity

A 21st-century professional accumulates tens of thousands of records across a lifetime—on paper and in digital form—including scans; photos; letters and postcards; medical and ID documents; tax and insurance files; business records and faxes; instructions, software, and manuals; datasets and papers; articles, clippings, and publications; notebooks and business cards; online/offline repositories, websites, emails, and social media; audio and video recordings; 3D models; physical artifacts; books; and magazines.

Yet most people lack the time, tools, and training to bring order to this sprawl. They don’t know what to keep, where to store it, how to describe it, or which standards ensure long-term access and reuse. Meanwhile, the need is immediate: to review and reference work, document a life, build timelines, and share results—before details fade.

Hiring an "assistant" is not a viable solution. Assistants need managing. Given that the 21st-century professional is clueless about most aspects of archiving, they are not in a position to manage an assistant. The result is that the professional remains clueless and the assistant remains unproductive.

## The Solution: Call upon an expert

A professional archivist is hired to work with the professional to archive their documents. The archivist has the skills, knowledge and experience to do the job. The archivist works closely with the professional.
