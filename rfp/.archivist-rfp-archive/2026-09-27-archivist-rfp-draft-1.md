### What I want

- **Phase 1, Assessment**: an on-site survey with me present, then an extent and condition report and a phased plan with cost ranges.
- **Phase 2, Triage and digitization**: an experienced archivist opens boxes and decides what each item is and where it goes (scan, specialist digitization, donation to an institution, sale, or recycling). Items are then batched out to the right specialists: sheet-feed, large-format photography, film scanning, 3D or object photography.
- **Phase 3, Management and hosting**: a landing zone for files, consistent naming, basic item or folder-level metadata, and transfer to long-term homes (Internet Archive, Permanent.org, GitHub) with integrity checks and redundancy. No ongoing hosting costs.
- **Placement**: help approaching Princeton (Norman Armour, Walter Lowrie), the Art Institute of Chicago (George Armour), and institutions interested in Koudacheff material and the artworks.
- **The key role**: I'm looking for someone to *manage* this, not do all of it: a lead archivist who sources and coordinates specialists from a broad network. I want to be an excellent client, not the project manager. Description and curation after digitization will be done largely by me, with AI help, over the rest of my life.
- **Values**: free and open source, freely available to future generations, and open to comments, reflections and connections. I'm also interested in what archiving and historiography can become in the age of AI.
