**The outcome: originals placed, open digital copies of everything**

I'm not trying to build an independent archive that I fund and staff. The aim is:

- **Digital images of everything worth keeping**, with good metadata and an open license (CC BY 4.0 / MIT), stored on free, long-lived public platforms such as the Internet Archive, Permanent.org, Wikimedia Commons and GitHub. They carry the hosting burden. I pay to get the material there.
- **New homes for the originals**, decided source by source:
  - Primary sources tied to a person who already has a collection go to that repository first. For example, Princeton for Norman Armour and Walter Lowrie.
  - Museum-quality art and objects go to museums as donations, with a qualified appraisal. Examples are the Art Institute of Chicago for the George Armour material and a Hermitage-related institution for the Koudacheff items.
  - Collectibles are sold to dealers or collectors.
  - Everything else is given away free where someone wants it. Otherwise it is recycled after imaging.
- **The end state:** I hold no physical objects, only the digital collection.

So in your terms the answer is placement, with one condition: an openly available digital copy. I know that condition may complicate talks with some institutions, and I'd welcome your advice on how to raise it.

**Who created the records**

I believe most of the material is primary sources created or accumulated by the relatives I name: their letters, photographs and business papers. There are some secondary materials, such as clippings and publications. I kept those mainly where I suspect no one else has preserved them well. I agree that my own papers are a separate collection.

**Art and objects**

Agreed, these need a museum professional or appraiser rather than an archivist. For the archivist I'd keep only photographing them and finding provenance papers in the boxes.

**Processing and digitization**

Your point about waiting until a receiving institution is involved is well taken. To be cautious, though, I'm planning on the assumption that nobody wants anything. That means it's up to me to start scanning everything. Doing so should also help organize, categorize and enrich the whole collection.
