Hello Theo,
Thank you for sharing the RFP and covering email. I’m glad to see you taking this step toward preserving the collections and making them accessible. I have some suggestions that I hope will help clarify the project for prospective archivists.


My main suggestion is that you **clarify the long-term outcome** you want before defining the full scope of work. Are you hoping to place the collections with established repositories that will take responsibility for preservation and public access, or to create an independent archive that you will fund and sustain? The draft combines these approaches, but they involve different responsibilities, infrastructure, and costs.

An individual Bay Area archivist could help assess the collections, identify potential repositories, and assist with placement. However, the full scope you describe goes well beyond what I would expect an individual consultant to provide, even with a strong network of specialists. It includes archival processing, specialized digitization and legacy media recovery, work with artworks and objects, and the establishment and continuing management of a digital archive. If you want to establish an independent archive, I would suggest exploring a firm such as Heritage Werks that offers broader staffing, technical infrastructure, digitization, and ongoing management. That would require a plan for funding and stewardship beyond the initial project and your lifetime.

If your primary goal is long-term public access through established repositories, my recommendation would be a collection assessment to support placement, followed by assistance identifying and approaching suitable institutions. The assessment would establish what is present: creators and provenance, content, dates, quantities, formats, condition, existing arrangement, research potential, and immediate preservation needs. It would also help determine whether the materials represent one collection or several, whether they should remain together, and which repositories might be interested. Whether you intend to donate or sell the materials would also inform that work.

Your descriptions convey the subject matter, but archivists will also want to understand who created and accumulated the records. For the family materials, are these primary sources created or accumulated by the relatives you name, materials about them, or a combination? Your own personal and professional papers would generally be considered a separate collection, depending on their relationship to the family materials.

Artworks and objects often fall outside archival scope unless they are integral to the collection. Their assessment and care may require a museum professional rather than an archivist.

If placement is the goal, I would leave processing and detailed digitization planning until a receiving institution is involved. It may prefer to undertake that work itself or specify how outside work should be completed to fit its systems and practices. You could discuss financing agreed preparatory work or contributing funds for a project archivist and digitization within the receiving institution. That could also provide a way for you to contribute contextual knowledge and inform description.

I think clarifying the intended home and long-term stewardship of the collections will make both the RFP and covering email easier to focus. It will also help prospective providers understand what you need, assess whether they can provide it, and offer realistic estimates.
