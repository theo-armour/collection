# GitHub Copilot Fusion: 50 Ways AI Will Transform Archiving

I’ve treated “Wikipedia-quality” as a reliable institutional, scholarly, or standards-based source—not Wikipedia itself. These are potential transformations, not claims that every archive already uses AI.

### Making collections readable

1. **Make printed records searchable at scale** by using machine learning to turn scans into text. [Library of Congress: AI](https://www.loc.gov/digital-strategy/artificial-intelligence/)
2. **Transcribe handwritten letters, registers, and manuscripts** so they can be searched and analyzed. [Open Research Europe: Transkribus and automated text recognition](https://open-research-europe.ec.europa.eu/articles/5-16)
3. **Create searchable transcripts of archival audio**, including recordings that have never been transcribed. [UNESCO: Artificial intelligence and documentary heritage](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000389844)
4. **Translate records across languages** to help more people discover collections beyond their own language. [UNESCO: Artificial intelligence and documentary heritage](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000389844)
5. **Recognize page layouts and separate text, images, and other regions** in complex documents. [Library of Congress: AI use cases](https://www.loc.gov/digital-strategy/ai-use-cases/)
6. **Extract information from historical maps**, such as place names and geographic references. [Library of Congress: AI](https://www.loc.gov/digital-strategy/artificial-intelligence/)
7. **Suggest corrections to imperfect machine transcription**, with people checking uncertain or consequential passages. [Library of Congress: Exploring Computational Description](https://labs.loc.gov/work/experiments/ECD/)
8. **Train recognition models for particular collections or handwriting styles**, rather than relying on one general-purpose model. [Open Research Europe: Transkribus and automated text recognition](https://open-research-europe.ec.europa.eu/articles/5-16)

### Describing and connecting records

9. **Suggest catalog keywords and subject headings** for archivists to review. [Library of Congress: Exploring Computational Description](https://labs.loc.gov/work/experiments/ECD/)
10. **Draft descriptions of images and other visual materials** to make under-described collections easier to discover. [Europeana: AI in relation to GLAMs](https://pro.europeana.eu/project/ai-in-relation-to-glams)
11. **Extract names, dates, and organizations** from documents to support indexing and discovery. [National Archives: AI strategic framework](https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/new-strategic-framework-artificial-intelligence)
12. **Suggest geographic tags for records** that mention places. [Library of Congress: AI](https://www.loc.gov/digital-strategy/artificial-intelligence/)
13. **Generate draft summaries** of records or collections as a starting point for human review. [UNESCO: Artificial intelligence and documentary heritage](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000389844)
14. **Enrich sparse catalogue records** with suggested descriptions and tags. [Europeana: AI in relation to GLAMs](https://pro.europeana.eu/project/ai-in-relation-to-glams)
15. **Link related people, places, and records** instead of leaving each catalogue entry isolated. [International Council on Archives: Records in Contexts Ontology](https://www.ica.org/standards/RiC/ontology)
16. **Represent complex relationships within collections** in formats that support richer discovery. [International Council on Archives: Records in Contexts Ontology](https://www.ica.org/standards/RiC/ontology)
17. **Find visually similar images** across large photographic collections. [Europeana: AI in relation to GLAMs](https://pro.europeana.eu/project/ai-in-relation-to-glams)
18. **Classify and tag visual materials** to help researchers browse collections by content. [Europeana: AI in relation to GLAMs](https://pro.europeana.eu/project/ai-in-relation-to-glams)
19. **Recommend related collection items** based on metadata and user activity. [Europeana: The Recommendation System](https://pro.europeana.eu/index.php/page/the-recommendation-system)
20. **Help institutions assess the quality of generated metadata** before it is added to public catalogues. [Library of Congress: Exploring Computational Description](https://labs.loc.gov/work/experiments/ECD/)

### Processing and preserving collections

21. **Help archivists prioritize appraisal** when the volume of digital records is too large for item-by-item review. [Digital Preservation Coalition: AI and digital preservation](https://www.dpconline.org/blog/dp-and-artificial-intelligence-a-4-point-plan)
22. **Support records classification and discovery** across large digital holdings. [National Archives: AI use cases](https://www.archives.gov/si)
23. **Help identify records that may need closer review before release**, including in FOIA workflows. [National Archives: AI strategic framework](https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/new-strategic-framework-artificial-intelligence)
24. **Flag possible personal information** for human review before records are made public. [National Archives: AI strategic framework](https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/new-strategic-framework-artificial-intelligence)
25. **Capture metadata during microfilm digitization** to reduce manual processing. [National Archives: AI strategic framework](https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/new-strategic-framework-artificial-intelligence)
26. **Improve access to large email collections** through machine-assisted processing and discovery. [ePADD](https://epaddproject.org/)
27. **Assist digital-preservation workflows**, such as appraisal, metadata extraction, and digital forensics. [Digital Preservation Coalition: AI and digital preservation](https://www.dpconline.org/blog/dp-and-artificial-intelligence-a-4-point-plan)
28. **Help identify preservation risks and processing priorities** across collections that cannot all be handled at once. [Digital Preservation Coalition: AI and digital preservation](https://www.dpconline.org/blog/dp-and-artificial-intelligence-a-4-point-plan)
29. **Document how AI-generated descriptions or transformations were made** so future users can assess them. [Digital Preservation Coalition: AI and digital preservation](https://www.dpconline.org/blog/dp-and-artificial-intelligence-a-4-point-plan)
30. **Help archives plan how to handle AI-generated material**, including questions of authenticity and accountability. [Digital Preservation Coalition: AI and digital preservation](https://www.dpconline.org/blog/dp-and-artificial-intelligence-a-4-point-plan)

### Changing how people use archives

31. **Enable natural-language searches** that let people ask questions in everyday terms. [National Archives: AI strategic framework](https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/new-strategic-framework-artificial-intelligence)
32. **Let researchers explore records through conversational interfaces**, while keeping the underlying sources available for verification. [National Archives: AI use cases](https://www.archives.gov/si)
33. **Make search more useful when researchers do not know the exact words used in a record** by adding semantic discovery. [Europeana: AI in relation to GLAMs](https://pro.europeana.eu/project/ai-in-relation-to-glams)
34. **Help genealogists find people across large record sets**, as demonstrated by NARA’s census-name identification work. [National Archives: AI strategic framework](https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/new-strategic-framework-artificial-intelligence)
35. **Make audiovisual collections more discoverable** by extracting and indexing their contents. [Library of Congress: AI use cases](https://www.loc.gov/digital-strategy/ai-use-cases/)
36. **Support research across collections**, including by surfacing names, topics, and relationships for further investigation. [Europeana: AI in relation to GLAMs](https://pro.europeana.eu/project/ai-in-relation-to-glams)
37. **Help users navigate large or unfamiliar collections** with recommendations and richer browsing tools. [Europeana: The Recommendation System](https://pro.europeana.eu/index.php/page/the-recommendation-system)
38. **Give more people access to records that previously required specialist language or transcription skills.** [UNESCO: Artificial intelligence and documentary heritage](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000389844)

### Responsibilities and risks

39. **Make human review a core part of AI-assisted description**, especially when errors could misidentify people or misrepresent records. [Library of Congress: Exploring Computational Description](https://labs.loc.gov/work/experiments/ECD/)
40. **Require archives to evaluate AI output for accuracy**, rather than treating generated descriptions as verified facts. [Digital Preservation Coalition: Using AI in community archives](https://www.dpconline.org/digipres/implement-digipres/community-archives-dp-toolkit/community-archives-ai)
41. **Make privacy and security part of tool selection**, particularly when working with sensitive or restricted material. [Digital Preservation Coalition: Using AI in community archives](https://www.dpconline.org/digipres/implement-digipres/community-archives-dp-toolkit/community-archives-ai)
42. **Require archives to consider copyright and permissions** before submitting collection material to AI services. [Digital Preservation Coalition: Using AI in community archives](https://www.dpconline.org/digipres/implement-digipres/community-archives-dp-toolkit/community-archives-ai)
43. **Expose bias in existing records and AI systems**, prompting archivists to scrutinize generated labels and summaries. [Digital Preservation Coalition: Using AI in community archives](https://www.dpconline.org/digipres/implement-digipres/community-archives-dp-toolkit/community-archives-ai)
44. **Respect Indigenous data sovereignty** by involving communities in decisions about their records and how AI may use them. [UNESCO: Indigenous data sovereignty and AI](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/new-report-and-guidelines-indigenous-data-sovereignty-artificial-intelligence-developments)
45. **Address gaps in language coverage**, since AI tools do not support all languages equally. [UNESCO: Artificial intelligence and documentary heritage](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000389844)
46. **Distinguish original records from AI-altered or AI-generated derivatives** to protect trust in archival evidence. [Digital Preservation Coalition: AI and digital preservation](https://www.dpconline.org/blog/dp-and-artificial-intelligence-a-4-point-plan)
47. **Preserve records of AI processes and decisions** where needed to support accountability. [National Archives: AI strategic framework](https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/new-strategic-framework-artificial-intelligence)
48. **Develop shared approaches for evaluating and documenting AI tools** used in preservation workflows. [Digital Preservation Coalition: AI and digital preservation](https://www.dpconline.org/blog/dp-and-artificial-intelligence-a-4-point-plan)
49. **Help smaller institutions access shared AI infrastructure and expertise**, rather than requiring every archive to build its own systems. [Open Research Europe: Transkribus and automated text recognition](https://open-research-europe.ec.europa.eu/articles/5-16)
50. **Shift archivists’ work toward oversight, judgment, and responsible access**, while using automation for repetitive tasks. [Digital Preservation Coalition: AI and digital preservation](https://www.dpconline.org/blog/dp-and-artificial-intelligence-a-4-point-plan)
