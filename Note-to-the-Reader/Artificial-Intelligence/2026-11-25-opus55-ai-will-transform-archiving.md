# 50 Ways AI Will Transform Archiving

Theo> Provide me with 50 bullets on how AI will transform archiving. Each bullet includes a Wikipedia-quality link to the source of the bullet.

Opus 5.5> Each link goes to the Wikipedia article on that topic, not to a primary study. All 50 links were checked and resolve, as of 2026-11-25. The forecasts are my synthesis.

## Getting text out of the record

1. OCR (optical character recognition) will be reliable enough that typed and printed collections become fully searchable by default, not by special project. — [Optical character recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_character_recognition)
2. Handwritten letters, ledgers and diaries will be transcribed by machine at scale. — [Handwriting recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handwriting_recognition)
3. Platforms like Transkribus let small archives train models on one hand or one script. — [Transkribus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transkribus)
4. Old scripts that only specialists can read will open up to general researchers. — [Palaeography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeography)
5. Oral histories and recorded interviews will be transcribed automatically, with timestamps. — [Whisper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whisper_(speech_recognition_system))
6. Decades of untranscribed oral-history tapes will finally become citable text. — [Oral history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_history)
7. Machine translation will open foreign-language holdings to readers who could never use them before. — [Machine translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_translation)

## Description and cataloguing

8. AI will draft subject headings and keywords, which clears cataloguing backlogs. — [Subject indexing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subject_indexing)
9. People, places, organisations and dates will be pulled from documents automatically. — [Named-entity recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Named-entity_recognition)
10. Extracted names will be linked to the right real-world person, not just matched as strings. — [Entity linking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entity_linking)
11. Those links will reach shared authority files, so collections connect across institutions. — [Virtual International Authority File](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_International_Authority_File)
12. AI will draft finding aids, the guides to a collection, for review by archivists. — [Finding aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finding_aid)
13. Box-level and folder-level summaries will be generated where none existed. — [Automatic summarization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_summarization)
14. Machine output will be fed into standard description formats instead of sitting in side systems. — [Encoded Archival Description](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encoded_Archival_Description)
15. Collections will be published as linked data, not just as isolated catalogue records. — [Linked data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linked_data)
16. Knowledge graphs will show the relationships across a collection, not just the contents of each item. — [Knowledge graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_graph)
17. Archives will contribute to and draw from open shared databases. — [Wikidata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikidata)

## Images, audio and video

18. Photo collections will be tagged by computer vision: objects, scenes, buildings, clothing, era. — [Computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision)
19. Face matching will identify people across unlabelled photographs, which raises consent questions. — [Facial recognition system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facial_recognition_system)
20. Low-resolution scans will be enhanced, with the risk of adding detail that was never there. — [Super-resolution imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super-resolution_imaging)
21. Damaged audio recordings will be cleaned and restored. — [Audio restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_restoration)
22. Film and video archives will be segmented and indexed by scene, speaker and on-screen text. — [Video content analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_content_analysis)
23. Sound and music archives will be searchable by melody, genre and instrument. — [Music information retrieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_information_retrieval)
24. Historical maps will be georeferenced automatically and layered onto modern geography. — [Georeferencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georeferencing)

## Recovering the unreadable

25. Carbonised scrolls are being read virtually without being unrolled. — [Vesuvius Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesuvius_Challenge)
26. Whole libraries once thought lost may become readable. — [Herculaneum papyri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herculaneum_papyri)
27. Models will suggest restorations of damaged inscriptions, along with likely dates and origins. — [Epigraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigraphy)
28. Hundreds of thousands of untranslated tablets will become accessible. — [Cuneiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform)
29. Scattered fragments will be matched back together across institutions. — [Cairo Geniza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_Geniza)
30. Whole city archives will be reconstructed as searchable "time machines." — [Venice Time Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice_Time_Machine)

## Appraisal, records and privacy

31. AI will help archivists decide what to keep from born-digital floods too large to read. — [Archival appraisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archival_appraisal)
32. Records management will shift from manual filing rules to automatic classification. — [Records management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Records_management)
33. Email collections of millions of messages will be sorted into the significant and the routine. — [Email archiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Email_archiving)
34. Methods from legal e-discovery will become standard archival tools. — [Electronic discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_discovery)
35. Government declassification backlogs will be reviewed with machine help. — [Declassification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declassification)
36. Automated redaction of sensitive and personal data will speed public release. — [Sanitization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitization_(classified_information))
37. Tension will grow between total recall and individuals' rights to erasure. — [Right to be forgotten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_be_forgotten)
38. Duplicates and near-duplicates will be found across huge storage systems. — [Data deduplication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_deduplication)

## Access and research

39. Search will work by meaning, not keyword, so researchers find documents that never use their terms. — [Semantic search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_search)
40. Reading-room chatbots will answer questions grounded in, and citing, the actual holdings. — [Retrieval-augmented generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrieval-augmented_generation)
41. Historians will "distant read" whole collections at once to find patterns. — [Distant reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distant_reading)
42. Machines will help attribute anonymous or disputed documents to their authors. — [Stylometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stylometry)
43. Genealogical records will be linked across censuses, parish registers and immigration files. — [Record linkage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_linkage)
44. Volunteer transcription will shift from typing to checking and correcting machine output. — [Zooniverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zooniverse)

## Digital preservation and the web

45. AI will identify obscure file formats and recommend migration before they become unreadable. — [PRONOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PRONOM)
46. Web archives will crawl more selectively and spot broken or incomplete captures. — [Web archiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_archiving)
47. Dead links in scholarship will be detected and repaired from archived copies. — [Link rot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_rot)

## New risks and responsibilities

48. Synthetic media will undermine the archive's role as trusted evidence, so verifying a record will become a core archival job. — [Deepfake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepfake)
49. Provenance standards will embed signed creation histories in digital files. — [C2PA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_for_Content_Provenance_and_Authenticity)
50. Machine-written descriptions can carry bias and invent facts, so archivists will need to audit them, not just accept them. — [Hallucination (AI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucination_(artificial_intelligence))
