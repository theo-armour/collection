## 2. Request for Proposal

# The Armour Archive: Collection Assessment and Archival Master Plan

*A request for proposals from Theo Armour, San Francisco*

### Overview

I'm looking for a Bay Area archivist to assess a large, mixed family and personal collection and write a master plan for it, and then, if we work well together, to lead the work that follows. I have spent my life as a designer. This archive is the design I most want to get right: free and open, useful to future generations, and a small experiment in what archiving and historiography can become in the age of AI.

### The collection

- **Extent:** about 200 linear feet, mostly in bank boxes, in two indoor storage units in San Francisco. There are also oversize architectural drawings.
- **Dates:** 1700s to the present, mostly from the 1970s onward.
- **Formats:** letters, postcards, photographs, slides, negatives, architectural drawings (some large-format), business records, publications, clippings, notebooks, manuals, software, audio, and data media.
- **Born-digital:** files on hard drives, Dropbox, Google Drive, and GitHub. Legacy media is likely in the boxes, extent unknown: floppies, Zip disks, CDs/DVDs, audio and video tapes, and old software such as AutoCAD.
- **Arrangement and condition:** order is quite mixed, with no reliable original order across boxes. There is some room in the units to open boxes. I know of no mold, pests, or water damage, but the survey should confirm this.
- **Art and objects:** about 30 works of art, several of museum quality, in a separate art storage facility, plus furniture and objets d'art. An older appraisal exists.

**Two threads run through it all.** One is family, with papers connected to Norman Armour, George Armour, Walter Lowrie, and the Koudacheff family. The other is my own career, which I half-seriously call "Three Ways Theo Helped Change the World." Whether or not that claim holds up, I believe the ordinary working records of ordinary people deserve keeping.

### Scope by phase

**Phase 1: Assessment and master plan (contracted under this RFP)**

An on-site survey with me present, followed by a written plan covering:

1. Inventory and condition assessment, at box or series level, with extent by format.
2. A recommended arrangement and cataloging approach, including suggested processing levels (MPLP-style where it makes sense) and a simple metadata scheme.
3. Digitization priorities, specifications (FADGI or equivalent), and estimated costs by format.
4. A digital preservation and access strategy, following OAIS principles in proportion to a personal archive. It should include an access policy (public / restricted / embargoed) and privacy, copyright, and ownership provisions.
5. A phased implementation schedule and budget.

**Phases 2–3: Please describe how you would lead these.**

- **Phase 2, triage and digitization:** an experienced archivist opens boxes, identifies what each item is, and decides where it goes: in-house scanning, specialist digitization, donation, sale, or recycling. Items are batched to the right specialists: sheet-feed scanning, large-format photography, film and slide scanning, AV transfer, disk imaging, object or 3D photography.
- **Phase 3, management and transfer:** a landing zone for files, consistent naming, item- or folder-level metadata, and transfer to long-term free homes (Internet Archive, Permanent.org, GitHub) with fixity checks and redundancy. No ongoing hosting costs.
- **Placement:** help approaching Princeton (Norman Armour, Walter Lowrie), the Art Institute of Chicago (George Armour), and institutions interested in Koudacheff material.

### Roles

- **You lead and coordinate.** You don't need to do everything yourself. I'm hoping for someone with a strong network of specialists.
- **I'm an engaged client.** I'll be present for the survey and available for decisions, but I don't want to be the project manager. I have one hand, so I'm hands-on only up to a point.
- **Disposition:** we agree rules up front, you apply them, and you bring exceptions to me. Nothing is recycled until I've had a chance to review it.
- **Access:** open by default. You flag sensitive material (third-party correspondence, living people, medical, financial, legal, and confidential business records) and recommend a policy. I make the final calls.
- **Art and objects:** in scope for photography, basic documentation, and finding provenance papers in the boxes. Monetary appraisal, including appraisal of donations for tax purposes, and art placement are referred to qualified appraisers and advisors.
- **Description after digitization:** largely mine, with AI help, over the years ahead.

### Deliverables (Phase 1)

- A written assessment and master plan covering items 1–5 above, in an editable format.
- A short debrief meeting to walk through recommendations.
- A Phase 2–3 cost estimate I can budget against.

### Budget and terms

- I haven't set a figure. Please estimate Phase 1 as **hours × rate**, showing survey days and report hours separately.
- For Phases 2–3, please include a **rate card**: hourly rates, per-item digitization rates by format, and how specialist costs are billed (pass-through or markup).
- I prefer hourly or per-item pricing to a lump sum, paid in phases or as a retainer. Later phases have no fixed end date.
- Quality over speed.
- Bay Area presence is required for the hands-on work.

### Selection criteria

- Experience with mixed personal and family collections, including fragile and oversize material.
- Competence with born-digital and legacy media, directly or through partners.
- A strong specialist network and a record of managing multi-vendor projects.
- Clear, warm communication, and interest in open access and in archiving in the age of AI.
- Reasonable, transparent pricing.

### Proposal requirements

Please send, in any format you like:

1. Your approach to Phase 1, and a sample or outline of a comparable assessment report.
2. Your Phase 1 estimate (hours × rate) and a Phases 2–3 rate card.
3. How you would lead Phases 2–3: your specialists, how you'd coordinate them, and how you'd handle legacy digital media (e.g. write-blocked imaging, BitCurator, AV transfer vendors).
4. Relevant experience with family, personal, architectural, or born-digital collections.
5. Two or three references.
6. Whether Phase 2 would need an off-site workspace, and what it would cost.

### Contact and timeline

- **Contact:** Theo Armour, t.armour@gmail.com
- **Proposals due:** [date, 4–6 weeks after posting]
- **Start:** upon agreement
- **Phase 1 report:** within 6–8 weeks of the on-site survey

Questions and an early conversation are very welcome. Thank you for reading. I hope this sounds like a project you'd enjoy.
